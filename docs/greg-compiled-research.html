Diet / protein summary (sent to Kim)

Mail / print for Kim: kim-seyfried-diet-letter.pdf · letter HTML (replaces the texts; diet only, no labs). Science brief for the team: what-we-are-treating.html — not the mailed letter.

Full verbatim text of the earlier note: email-to-kim-diet-protein.md

One-line takeaway: do not replace carbs with huge amounts of meat. Replace most of those calories with healthy fat, keep protein adequate/moderate, and protect muscle.

Seyfried / metabolic framing as written

  • Very low carbs and glucose. Healthy fats are the primary calorie source. Protein moderate — enough for muscle, not the dominant calories. Not huge portions of meat.
  • Keep carbohydrates and glucose very low.
  • Use healthy fats as the primary calorie source.
  • Give enough protein to maintain muscle without making protein the dominant calorie source.
  • Prefer whole-food protein when tolerated — eggs, fish, clean meats — generally paired with fat.
  • Bone broth is a reasonable option when eating is difficult; add butter to raise fat without more protein or carbs.
  • If a shake is needed because food intake is too low: clean unsweetened pea or rice protein, essentially no added sugar, very low carb.
  • Other fats named: olive oil through the day, butter in tea, avocado, MCT oil, ghee/butter.
  • Track glucose + ketones (GKI) so the diet is measured, not assumed.
  • If weight or muscle drops, reassess protein/calories rather than rigidly restricting them.

Glutamine clarification (important)

The reason to keep protein moderate is not that dietary protein can simply eliminate or control glutamine. The body can produce glutamine itself. The practical goal is enough protein to preserve lean muscle during treatment while still keeping glucose and GKI under control.

GKI (Glucose-Ketone Index) is the Seyfried measurement of the relationship between blood glucose and ketones.
