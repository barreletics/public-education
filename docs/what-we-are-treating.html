The disease, in plain language

It has already spread. Confirmed metastatic sites in the compilation: liver, lymph nodes, and multiple bones. Small lung nodules were present but nonspecific / non-FDG-avid — not confirmed metastases.

Despite extensive liver involvement, the initial labs sent to Dr. Max were still fairly preserved: AST 28, ALT 35, Alk Phos 73, bilirubin 0.6, creatinine 1.08, WBC 5.1, Hgb 14.1, platelets 169; CEA 33.5.

Why diet is an adjunct — not a cure claim

Conventional treatment is already underway. The metabolic diet is a research adjunct under clinician guidance, not a substitute for that treatment and not a claim that we are curing this cancer with food.

  • Greg has not started the ketogenic / metabolic diet.
  • The paper below is late-stage experimental glioblastoma in mice, not human sinonasal high-grade NEC.
  • In that paper, the strongest reported effect was the diet plus a glutamine-antagonist drug (DON). Diet alone was weaker. We are not starting DON or any other drug protocol from this work.
  • The authors themselves note the mice were not treated with surgery, radiation, or standard chemotherapy, and that it is unclear whether a similar response would be seen in people using this approach with current standard of care.
This page does not start FenBen, ivermectin, high-dose melatonin, or any supplement stack. Those remain research questions for clinicians. See the compiled research page for what was asked — not ordered.

The paper the Kim diet note is using

Mukherjee P, Augur ZM, Li M, Hill C, Greenwood B, Domin MA, Kondakci G, Narain NR, Kiebish MA, Bronson RT, Arismendi-Morillo G, Chinopoulos C, Seyfried TN. Therapeutic benefit of combining calorie-restricted ketogenic diet and glutamine targeting in late-stage experimental glioblastoma. Communications Biology. 2019;2:200. Published 29 May 2019.

DOI: 10.1038/s42003-019-0455-x  ·  PMCID: PMC6541653  ·  PMID: 31149644

Corresponding author: Thomas N. Seyfried (Boston College). Open access, CC BY 4.0. Read from PMC; do not rely on memory of “the Seyfried keto paper.”

What the paper actually claims

These are findings from the article, not inferences about Greg.

  • Model: two syngeneic orthotopic mouse GBM models (VM-M3 and CT-2A), treated late after implantation.
  • Fuels named: glucose (glycolysis) and glutamine (glutaminolysis) are described as the major fuels driving GBM growth and invasion in this framework.
  • Diet (KD-R): a calorically restricted ketogenic diet — low carbohydrate, high fat — used to lower blood glucose and raise ketone bodies (β-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate). The commercial diet they used (KetoGEN) was 2.1% of calories from carbohydrate, 8.7% from protein, and 89.2% from fat. Intake was restricted to hold about a 15–18% body-weight reduction in the mice.
  • Not unrestricted keto: they did not include an ad-libitum ketogenic-diet arm. They state that unrestricted ketogenic diets can cause insulin insensitivity and weight gain.
  • Glutamine targeting was a drug, not a grocery list: 6-diazo-5-oxo-L-norleucine (DON) is a glutamine antagonist given intraperitoneally at low, intermittent doses. The paper does not claim that dietary protein restriction eliminates glutamine. It says the body / host systems need glutamine (immune system and gut), so glutamine targeting “must be done strategically.”
  • Press-pulse: they explicitly map this experiment onto Seyfried’s press-pulse idea — KD-R as the press (chronic metabolic stress / lower glucose, higher ketones) and DON as the pulse (acute glutamine restriction). That is a diet-plus-drug design, not diet-as-cure.
  • GKI: they measured blood glucose and ketones (Keto-Mojo) and reported the Glucose-Ketone Index. They treat a lower GKI as greater metabolic stress on the tumour cells. GKI fell on KD-R; adding DON did not further change GKI versus KD-R alone.
  • What improved in mice: KD-R + DON reduced tumour bioluminescence more than either alone (they call this synergistic), reduced edema / hemorrhage / inflammation markers (TNF-α, Iba-1), and extended survival versus untreated controls. KD-R alone had a smaller effect (they report a 19% lower photon score versus unrestricted standard diet in one VM-M3 experiment).
  • Muscle / host cost: they report no noticeable DON toxicity until late in the study, when mild muscle loss was seen in some mice. That is a reason not to treat “restrict protein” as a goal.
  • Glutamine is not dietarily erased: they note evidence that glutamine supplementation can be protective for patients, that tumour glutamine use is dissipative, and that supplementation might not increase tumour growth — and they say more preclinical work is needed. That is the opposite of “cut protein to remove glutamine.”

How this supports the Kim diet note — and where it stops

The Kim letter is a caregiver diet frame: not high-protein carnivore; fat as primary calories; protein moderate to protect muscle; glutamine not eliminated by diet; measure glucose and ketones rather than assuming “keto.” PMC6541653 supports that frame only in the following, limited ways:

Kim diet noteWhat PMC6541653 actually shows
Not a high-protein carnivore diet The KD-R they fed was ~9% protein / ~89% fat / ~2% carbohydrate by calories. Protein is present and modest, not the calorie engine.
Fat as the primary calorie source Matches the diet composition they used. Ketone bodies are presented as an alternative fuel for normal cells under glucose restriction, not as something tumour cells ferment well in their model.
Protein moderate; protect muscle Protein was a small calorie share, not zero. They still watched body weight daily. Late mild muscle loss under DON is a caution against aggressive protein restriction. The paper does not give a human gram target — that remains a question for Dr. Max.
Glutamine is not “turned off” by cutting dietary protein Glutamine was targeted with DON. The authors say the host can make glutamine, that glutamine matters for immune function and gut, and that diet does not substitute for that pharmacology. They do not recommend eliminating glutamine from food.
Measure glucose + ketones (GKI); do not assume a food is “keto” They treat GKI as the blood biomarker of the KD-R. A ketogenic menu is not the endpoint; the glucose-to-ketone relationship is.
Press-pulse language in the broader Seyfried framing This paper cites press-pulse and uses KD-R as press, DON as pulse. The Kim letter is the press (diet) only. No pulse drug is being started from this document.
Do not stretch the paper. It does not study neuroendocrine carcinoma. It does not show that a human “keto” grocery list manages metastatic small-cell NEC. It does not justify unrestricted high-fat eating, protein starvation, or a DON / glutaminase-inhibitor protocol. It supports only this: if the family uses Seyfried’s metabolic framing as an adjunct, the published KD-R is high-fat, moderate-protein, very-low-carb, calorie-aware, and judged by glucose and ketones — not by meat volume.
