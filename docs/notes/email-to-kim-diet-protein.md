Hi Kim,

I wanted to pull the diet piece into one simple summary because I know I sent you a lot of information.

The main takeaway from the Seyfried/metabolic approach is that this is **not meant to be a high-protein carnivore diet**. The goal is to keep carbohydrates and glucose very low, use **healthy fats as the primary calorie source**, and provide enough protein to maintain muscle without making protein the dominant source of calories.

For protein, the priority should be **whole-food sources when he can tolerate them** — eggs, fish, clean meats, etc. — generally paired with healthy fats. I think you’re on the right track with **bone broth**, especially if eating is difficult. You can also **add butter to the bone broth** to bring the fat up without adding more protein or carbs.

If he needs a protein shake because he simply cannot get enough food in, we can look for a very clean **unsweetened pea or rice protein with essentially no added sugar and very low carbohydrates**.

One clarification from what I sent earlier: the reason to keep protein moderate is **not that dietary protein can simply eliminate or control glutamine**. The body can produce glutamine itself. The more practical goal is to give him **enough protein to preserve lean muscle**, particularly during treatment, while still keeping glucose and the GKI under control. We definitely do not want him losing muscle because we restricted protein too aggressively.

The **GKI (Glucose-Ketone Index)** is basically the measurement Seyfried uses to look at the relationship between blood glucose and ketones. That is why checking glucose and ketones can be useful — it gives us something objective to follow rather than just assuming a food is “keto.”

So, very simply:

* **Carbs/sugar:** keep very low.
* **Protein:** moderate and sufficient to protect muscle — not huge portions, but not underfeeding him either.
* **Fat:** this becomes the main calorie source.
* **Bone broth:** good option when appetite is limited; **add butter** if he tolerates it.
* **Olive oil:** use **throughout the day** and drizzle it onto whatever food he is able to eat.
* **Tea:** adding **butter** is another easy way to increase fat intake without increasing protein or carbs.
* **Other fats:** avocado, MCT oil, ghee/butter, etc.
* **Protein powder if needed:** clean, unsweetened, low-carb pea or rice protein.
* **Track glucose + ketones:** so we can see how the diet is actually affecting him.
* **Weight/muscle matter:** if he starts losing weight or muscle, protein/calories need to be reassessed rather than rigidly restricting them.

I think **Dr. Max can help us fill in the biggest gap — exactly how much protein and total calories he needs based on his weight, appetite, treatment and whether he is maintaining muscle.**

The simplest way I’m thinking about it is: **don’t replace carbs with huge amounts of meat. Replace most of those calories with healthy fat, keep protein adequate/moderate, and protect his muscle.**
