Therapeutic benefit of combining calorie-restricted ketogenic diet and glutamine targeting in late-stage experimental glioblastoma. *Communications Biology*. 2019;2:200. Published 29 May 2019. doi:[10.1038/s42003-019-0455-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-019-0455-x). PMCID: [PMC6541653](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6541653/). ## Takeaways the Kim letter should use

1. **Not high-protein carnivore.** The calorie-restricted ketogenic diet (KD-R) they fed was about **2.1% carbohydrate, 8.7% protein, 89.2% fat** by calories (KetoGEN).
2. **Fat is the calorie engine.** Protein is present and modest — enough to be a macronutrient, not the bulk of energy.
3. **They restricted calories in mice** (about 15–18% body-weight reduction) and did **not** test an unrestricted ketogenic diet; they say ad-libitum keto can cause insulin insensitivity and weight gain.
4. **Glutamine was not removed from food.** It was targeted with the glutamine-antagonist drug DON. The authors say the host needs glutamine (immune system, gut) and that targeting must be strategic. Do not tell Kim to starve protein to “control glutamine.”
5. **The body makes glutamine.** The paper discusses host glutamine production and even notes that glutamine supplementation might be protective and might not increase tumour growth — more preclinical work needed.
6. **Measure glucose and ketones (GKI).** They used a meter (Keto-Mojo) and treat a lower Glucose-Ketone Index as the biomarker of the diet, not the assumption that a food is “keto.”
7. **Press-pulse in this paper is diet + drug.** KD-R is the press; DON is the pulse. The Kim letter is diet-only. Do not imply a pulse drug is being started.
8. **This is mouse glioblastoma**, not Greg’s sinonasal high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma. Diet-alone effects were smaller than diet + DON. Not a human cure claim; not medical advice.
9. **Protect muscle.** They weighed mice daily; late mild muscle loss appeared in some DON-treated mice. If weight or muscle drops, reassess protein and calories rather than restricting harder.
