## 3. What “therapeutic keto” looks like (as sent)

To keep blood glucose low while avoiding a glutamine overload, a therapeutic ketogenic diet behaves very differently than a standard carnivore diet [PMC6541653]:

- **Strictly limited protein:** Protein is tightly restricted—often to just 0.8 to 1.2 grams per kilogram of lean body mass per day. This provides just enough protein to prevent muscle wasting without leaving any excess for the tumor to exploit.
- **The GKI rule:** Dr. Seyfried measures success using the Glucose-Ketone Index (GKI) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4367849/). To achieve a therapeutic GKI of 1.0 or lower (where blood ketones are higher than or equal to blood glucose), the diet must be roughly 80% to 90% of calories from pure fat.
- **The food choices:** Instead of large plates of steak or chicken, a therapeutic cancer diet relies heavily on plant and animal fats that contain virtually zero protein or carbohydrates. This includes macadamia nut oil, pure MCT oil, avocado oil, extra virgin olive oil, and high-fat ghee or butter [PMC6541653]. Protein is treated almost like a side dish or garnish to keep glutamine levels down.

Fat is key.
