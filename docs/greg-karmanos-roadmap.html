Greg's Situation — Plain Language

  • Primary tumor: Sinus / paranasal area — neuroendocrine carcinoma, high grade (aggressive cells, fast-dividing)
  • Spread: Liver and pelvis = stage IV metastatic disease
  • What that means: Systemic (whole-body) treatment is the main focus, not surgery alone
  • Good news: High-grade NEC often responds initially to platinum + etoposide chemotherapy (same class used for small cell lung cancer)
  • Hard truth: This is rare. Almost none of the fenbendazole Facebook success stories are this exact cancer — use them for hope and protocol ideas, not as proof it works for NEC

Questions for the Oncologist (write answers in the blanks)

Diagnosis & staging

  1. Confirm: Is this poorly differentiated / high-grade NEC (Grade 3)? What is the Ki-67 percentage?
  2. Is this small-cell type or large-cell neuroendocrine carcinoma?
  3. Exact staging — liver: how many lesions, size? Pelvis: bone, lymph nodes, or soft tissue?
  4. Was a DOTATATE PET (Gallium-68) scan done? (Determines if Lutathera PRRT is an option — often negative in high-grade NEC, but must check)
  5. Any genetic/molecular testing planned? (e.g. MGMT, TP53, next-gen sequencing for trial options)

Treatment plan

  1. First-line recommendation — is it carboplatin + etoposide (or cisplatin + etoposide)? How many cycles?
  2. Is radiation planned to the sinus primary for local control?
  3. Any role for liver-directed therapy (embolization, ablation) if liver mets are limited?
  4. Is Lutathera (PRRT) being considered, or ruled out based on receptor status?
  5. Are there clinical trials at Karmanos for high-grade extrapulmonary NEC?
  6. Expected timeline — when does treatment start?

Metabolic / integrative (ask calmly, not confrontationally)

  1. We plan strict ketogenic diet and glucose-ketone tracking (GKI). Any concerns with chemo?
  2. Are you open to us adding repurposed antiparasitics (fenbendazole/ivermectin) as adjuncts? Any drug interaction concerns with etoposide/platinum?
  3. Which supplements are safe vs. must avoid during chemo?
  4. Who on the team helps with nutrition during treatment?
  5. How do we reach you between visits if side effects spike?

Critical framing for Greg: The metabolic protocol (keto + antiparasitics) is meant to make chemo work better by starving tumor fuel and reducing the metabolic shield — not to replace carboplatin/etoposide. High-grade NEC needs systemic chemo.

Phase 3 — Weeks 5–12 (commit, don't quit early)

Why This Cancer Type Needs a Different Lens

  • Rare disease: Sinonasal high-grade NEC is uncommon — Karmanos NET team is the right expertise
  • Fast-growing: High Ki-67 = chemo-sensitive but also recurs quickly if under-treated — don't delay SOC
  • Liver mets: Metabolic therapy targets systemic fuel supply; chemo targets dividing cells — complementary theory
  • No FBZ case series for this exact cancer: Community data is breast/colon/lung/prostate heavy — use protocol logic, not cancer-type match
  • NED is still the goal: No Evidence of Disease on scans — what Facebook groups celebrate
