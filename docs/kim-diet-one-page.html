What I already sent you about protein

Kim — this can help for the protein. I researched it and sent a lot of pieces by text. This is that same information, in order, so you do not have to hunt through messages.

The diet is not a big-meat / carnivore diet. Most of his calories should come from fat. Protein is a side dish — enough to protect muscle, not a huge plate. Carbs and sugar stay very low. Fat is the key.

1. You’re already doing the right thing

Bone broth is on the right track, especially when solid food is hard. You can add butter to the broth if he tolerates it — that adds fat without adding more protein or carbs. Butter in tea works the same way.

2. Protein — what I meant

  • First choice: real food when he can eat it — eggs, fish, or a modest amount of clean meat. Not a large steak plate.
  • Always pair protein with fat — olive oil, avocado, butter, or ghee. That slows how fast it hits him and is how this diet is supposed to work.
  • Bone broth (or a plain unsweetened bone-broth protein, if you ever use a powder) is a gentler option than a whey / dairy shake.
  • A protein shake only if he cannot get enough food — muscle wasting, or he simply cannot eat. Then: unsweetened pea or rice protein, no sugar, almost no carbs. I can check brands I know. Do not start a shake just because we mentioned one.

3. Fat is most of the plate

In the research diet, most calories — roughly 80–90% — come from fat, not from meat. Use fats that are basically just fat: extra-virgin olive oil, butter, ghee, avocado, avocado oil, MCT oil, macadamia oil. Drizzle olive oil on whatever he can eat, through the day.

Think of the egg or the fish as the side. The oil, butter, and avocado are the meal.

4. What to skip

  • Sugar, soda, juice, bread, pasta, rice
  • Replacing those with huge amounts of meat
  • Whey or sweetened protein drinks

5. The clarification I sent after — please keep this one

I went back and checked. “Moderate protein” is right, but not because eating less meat will turn off glutamine. The body can make glutamine itself. So we do not starve him of protein to try to control that.

The real goal: enough protein to keep muscle, while keeping sugar/carbs low, and using fat as the main calories. If he is losing weight or muscle, we feed more — we do not cut protein harder to chase a number.

6. GKI — only so the word isn’t a mystery

GKI means glucose-ketone index. It is just a way to look at blood sugar next to blood ketones, so we are not guessing that a food is “keto.” You do not have to hit a target today. If we start tracking, Dr. Max can tell us what to aim for.

7. What you do not have to figure out

Exact grams of protein and total calories — that is Dr. Max, from Greg’s weight, appetite, treatment, and whether he is holding muscle. I think Max can fill in those gaps. Your job is the plate: fat first, protein as a side, bone broth when eating is hard, no sugar.
